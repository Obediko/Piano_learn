# Ear Training Track, Weeks 1-26

The goal is not perfect pitch. The goal is functional hearing: finding tonic, hearing direction, recognising chord quality, tracking bass movement and eventually working out simple songs with less tutorial dependence.

## Rules

- Sing or hum before checking on the keyboard whenever possible.
- Use short tests, 5-10 minutes, almost every day.
- Do not guess endlessly. Make one guess, check, then repeat correctly.
- Log score out of 10 when a task can be tested.

## Weeks 1-4: direction and home

### Week 1
Higher or lower?
- play one note, then a second note;
- say `higher`, `lower` or `same` before looking;
- 10 trials, target 8/10 by Saturday.

### Week 2
Tonic/home feeling.
- play C-F-G-C slowly;
- stop on G and notice incompleteness;
- resolve to C;
- 10 trials where you label `home` versus `not home`.

### Week 3
Step versus skip.
- compare seconds and thirds/fourths/fifths;
- sing two notes before playing them;
- target 7/10.

### Week 4
Major versus minor.
- compare C/Cm, F/Fm, G/Gm;
- target: 8/10 self-generated random order by end of week.

## Weeks 5-8: scale degree feel

### Week 5
Sing 1-2-3-2-1 and 1-3-5 in G and C.

### Week 6
Hear 1, 4 and 5 as destinations.
- play tonic then 4;
- tonic then 5;
- 5 then 1;
- describe 5->1 as tension resolving home.

### Week 7
Inversion awareness.
- hear same C major chord in different inversions;
- answer `same chord quality or different chord?`;
- do not worry about inversion naming by ear yet.

### Week 8
Bass-direction test.
- play roots D-G-A-B and other simple sequences;
- identify up/down/same movement.

## Weeks 9-13: progression hearing

### Week 9
Compare 1->4, 1->5, 1->6 in C and G.
- hum the bass destination before playing.

### Week 10
Major/minor diatonic chord quality.
- in C, randomly play I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi;
- label major/minor only;
- target 8/10.

### Week 11
Phrase-ending feel.
- play 5->1 versus 4->1;
- identify which feels more strongly resolved.

### Week 12
Bass roots under a familiar chorus.
- pause after each phrase and sing likely next bass note before checking.

### Week 13
Mini transposition ear test.
- sing tonic of a known chorus;
- find it in C, G or F without looking at a tutorial.

## Weeks 14-17: chord colour

### Week 14
Triad vs dominant 7 vs major 7.
- focus first on `plain`, `tense/bluesy`, `soft/colourful` character;
- naming can follow.

### Week 15
Hear scalar walk-up/walk-down.
- sing 1-2-3-4 before playing;
- then identify direction from audio or self-played patterns.

### Week 16
2-5-1 function.
- hear 2 as preparation, 5 as tension, 1 as release;
- sing roots before playing.

### Week 17
Suspension colour.
- compare major triad, sus4 resolving to major, add9;
- identify which version contains unresolved tension.

## Weeks 18-22: functional song learning

### Week 18
Passing chord discrimination.
- distinguish destination chord from connecting chord;
- always sing destination bass notes first.

### Week 19
Melody-top-note hearing.
- play melody plus harmony;
- sing only the top note;
- verify melody remains perceptually clear.

### Week 20
Transposition hearing.
- play same progression in C, G, F, D;
- recognise identical harmonic function despite pitch change.

### Week 21
First real by-ear assignment.
Before using any tutorial:
1. find tonic;
2. find first melody note;
3. find bass note of first 4-8 bars;
4. guess number progression;
5. only then check source.

Score yourself:
- tonic correct = 2 points
- opening melody direction = 2
- first bass root = 2
- at least half chord roots correct = 2
- cadence/home recognised = 2

### Week 22
Repeat by-ear protocol on a simple gospel chorus. Target at least 6/10 before checking.

## Weeks 23-26: independence

### Week 23
Hear chord enrichment.
- compare triad versus 7/add9/sus;
- state what changed by note or function after checking.

### Week 24
Arrangement listening.
- record your own sparse and full versions;
- identify whether melody remains audible and whether bass movement makes sense.

### Week 25
Memory from sound.
- listen to a familiar piece without touching keyboard;
- mentally predict first chord, tonic and first phrase direction;
- then test.

### Week 26
Final ear test.
For two familiar but not currently practised simple choruses:
- find tonic;
- find opening melody note;
- identify likely 1/4/5/6 bass movement;
- reproduce first phrase before checking any source.

## What counts as success after six months

You do not need to name every chord instantly by ear. Success means you can usually locate tonic, tell major/minor, hear basic bass direction, recognise strong 5->1 resolution, and work out the skeleton of an easy familiar chorus before opening YouTube.
