# Technique Map, Weeks 1-26

This is the technical spine of the course. It prevents the repertoire track from becoming chord-copying without physical control.

## General fingering conventions

Finger numbers: thumb=1, index=2, middle=3, ring=4, little finger=5.

### Major-scale fingering groups

For C, G, D, A, E major:
- Right hand ascending: 1-2-3-1-2-3-4-5
- Left hand ascending: 5-4-3-2-1-3-2-1

For F major:
- Right hand ascending: 1-2-3-4-1-2-3-4
- Left hand ascending: 5-4-3-2-1-3-2-1

For Bb major, beginner one-octave:
- Right hand ascending: 2-1-2-3-1-2-3-4
- Left hand ascending: 3-2-1-4-3-2-1-3

For Eb major, beginner one-octave:
- Right hand ascending: 3-1-2-3-4-1-2-3
- Left hand ascending: 3-2-1-4-3-2-1-3

For Ab major, beginner one-octave:
- Right hand ascending: 3-4-1-2-3-1-2-3
- Left hand ascending: 3-2-1-4-3-2-1-3

For B, Db and Gb/F# major, consult the named scale video/reference before speed work. The goal in this six-month course is correct relaxed one-octave execution, not virtuoso scale velocity.

## Technique tempo standard

All tempo goals are ceilings, not deadlines. Start slower if needed.

- Weeks 1-4: 50-60 bpm, quarter notes
- Weeks 5-8: 55-70 bpm
- Weeks 9-13: 60-75 bpm
- Weeks 14-17: 60-80 bpm
- Weeks 18-22: 65-85 bpm
- Weeks 23-26: 70-90 bpm in priority keys if relaxed and accurate

Never raise tempo with tension.

## Weeks 1-4: keyboard control

### Week 1
- five-finger C-D-E-F-G pattern, each hand separately;
- 5 repetitions ascending/descending per hand;
- C-F-G root-position triads, 10 slow changes each pair;
- target: locate C/F/G within 2 seconds without counting keys.

### Week 2
- C major scale, one octave, hands separately;
- thumb-under isolation: E-F and A-B-C transitions;
- C-F-G-C at 50-60 bpm, one chord per 4 beats;
- target: 3 clean C scales each hand.

### Week 3
- contrary-motion five-finger C patterns;
- hold left-hand C while right hand plays five notes;
- interval drills 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th;
- target: maintain relaxed left hand while right hand moves.

### Week 4
- C major scale at 60 bpm hands separately;
- C-Am-F-G chord cycle;
- major/minor triad conversion: C/Cm, F/Fm, G/Gm;
- target: 20 chord changes without losing count.

## Weeks 5-8: new keys and inversions

### Week 5
- G major scale;
- G-C-D-Em construction;
- 1-5-6-4 in G;
- target: G scale hands separately + progression 3 clean rounds.

### Week 6
- F major scale;
- F-Bb-C-Dm;
- sustain pedal coordination on slow changes;
- target: no overlapping mud across 8 consecutive chord changes.

### Week 7
- C, F, G triads in root, 1st inversion, 2nd inversion;
- nearest-position C-F-G voice-leading;
- target: name inversion and play it without diagram.

### Week 8
- D major scale;
- D-G-A-Bm;
- block-chord rhythmic repeats;
- target: 1-5-6-4 in D at minimum 60 bpm, whole-note changes.

## Weeks 9-13: accompaniment mechanics

### Week 9
- root-fifth left-hand pattern in C/G/F;
- right-hand closest inversions;
- target: 60 seconds of 1-5-6-4 without stopping.

### Week 10
- diatonic triads in C and G;
- build each from scale, not diagrams;
- target: random callout drill, 80% correct on first attempt.

### Week 11
- A major scale;
- simple broken-chord right-hand pattern on A-D-E-F#m;
- target: dynamic contrast without speeding up.

### Week 12
- Bb major scale;
- left-hand root, root-fifth and octave options;
- target: choose one pattern and keep pulse for 60 seconds.

### Week 13
- six-key review: C/G/D/A/F/Bb;
- one octave each hand separately;
- progression circuit in C/G/F/D;
- target: no more than one restart across the circuit.

## Weeks 14-17: seventh-chord control

### Week 14
- C7, Cmaj7, Cm7; then G7, Fmaj7, Dm7, Am7;
- target: construct from chord spelling, not memorised shape alone.

### Week 15
- left-hand scalar walk 1-2-3-4 and 4-3-2-1;
- right hand holds simple harmony;
- target: bass walk remains even and deliberate.

### Week 16
- 2-5-1 in C/F/G;
- use simple close-position right hand + left roots;
- target: three consecutive clean cadences in each key.

### Week 17
- sus2/sus4/add9 colour tones;
- keep melody/top note audible;
- target: compare plain vs enriched voicing and explain added note.

## Weeks 18-22: coordination and independence

### Week 18
- one passing movement between known destination chords;
- target: destination timing unchanged by decoration.

### Week 19
- melody plus inner chord tones, right hand;
- left hand roots only;
- target: melody is clearly louder/uppermost.

### Week 20
- same easy progression in C/G/F/D;
- target: transpose by numbers without writing every letter first.

### Week 21
- ear-led melody finding;
- play short phrase with one finger first, then assign efficient fingering;
- target: identify tonic before checking a source.

### Week 22
- inversions + one tasteful passing movement;
- target: 1-5-6-4 in G at 70 bpm if clean.

## Weeks 23-26: consolidation

### Week 23
- seventh/add9/sus review;
- major/minor triads in all keys from formula;
- target: explain each enriched chord structurally.

### Week 24
- intro/body/ending transitions;
- target: no pause between sections.

### Week 25
- pressure practice: start songs from middle sections;
- target: recover after one intentional stop without returning to beginning.

### Week 26
- all 12 major scales at least hands separately, one octave;
- priority keys C/G/D/A/F/Bb strongest;
- chord and progression survey;
- target: technique supports performance rather than becoming the performance.

## Hand-independence progression

1. Hold one left note while right plays 5-finger pattern.
2. Left whole notes + right melody.
3. Left roots on beats 1/3 + right chords.
4. Left root-fifth + right block chords.
5. Left root/fifth + right inversions.
6. Left moving bass + right held harmony.
7. Left accompaniment + right melody/chords.

Do not jump ahead if the earlier layer is unstable.
