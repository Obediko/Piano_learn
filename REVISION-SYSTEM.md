# Revision and Retention System

New material is useless if old skills disappear. This system forces spaced retrieval across technique, harmony and repertoire.

## The 1-2-4 rule

Every new skill or piece is revisited:
- 1 week later;
- 2 weeks later;
- 4 weeks later.

After that, it enters monthly maintenance.

## Weekly maintenance slots

### Monday
5 minutes: one scale from the previous month.

### Tuesday
5 minutes: one old progression in a different key.

### Wednesday
5 minutes: one old chorus from memory.

### Thursday
5 minutes: one old ear-training category.

### Friday
10 minutes: one older performance-ready piece.

### Saturday
Current piece + one recent piece + one older piece.

## Rotating scale pool

### Weeks 1-4
C

### Weeks 5-8
C, G, F, D

### Weeks 9-13
C, G, F, D, A, Bb

### Weeks 14-17
Maintain six priority keys while adding technical harmony.

### Weeks 18-22
Add E and Eb gradually if comfortable.

### Weeks 23-26
Survey all 12 major scales, prioritising clean execution over speed.

## Progression pool

Keep these alive once introduced:
- 1-4-5-1
- 1-5-6-4
- 6-4-1-5
- 1-6-4-5
- 2-5-1

Saturday progression circuit:
1. choose two progressions;
2. choose two keys;
3. play each three times without stopping;
4. use closest inversions on the final repetition.

## Repertoire pool

Keep four labels in `PROGRESS.md`:
- Learning
- Basic
- Secure
- Performance-ready

A piece can move backward if neglected. That is normal.

## Monthly retrieval day

At each monthly exam, randomly select:
- one scale not practised that week;
- one progression from an earlier month;
- one chorus older than four weeks;
- one ear task from an earlier stage.

This checks retention rather than short-term rehearsal.

## Interleaving rule

Do not spend the whole hour on one skill unless repairing a serious weakness.

Normal practice should mix:
- technique;
- harmony;
- rhythm;
- ear;
- repertoire.

This makes practice harder but learning more durable.

## Memory test for songs

A song is not secure until you can:
- start from the beginning;
- start from a middle section;
- name the main number progression;
- recover after one mistake;
- play without the tutorial.

## Monthly clean-up

At the end of each month:
1. archive nothing yet;
2. choose the three weakest skills;
3. choose only one as next month's primary repair target;
4. keep the other two in 5-minute maintenance slots;
5. choose three strongest songs for the performance-ready pool.

This prevents the plan from becoming overloaded with unfinished repairs.
