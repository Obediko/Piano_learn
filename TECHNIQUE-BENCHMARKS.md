# Piano Technique Benchmarks — Measurable Without Chasing Speed

Technique targets make progress observable while protecting relaxation, pulse and musical control.

## General rule
Never increase tempo if there is tension, repeated wrong fingering, broken pulse or loss of coordination. Clean repetition beats speed.

## Benchmark families
### Scales
Weeks 1–4: C major hands separately, clean at 60 bpm one note/click.
Weeks 5–8: C/G/F/D hands separately, 60–72 bpm.
Weeks 9–13: add A/Bb; priority keys fluent at 72–84 bpm.
Weeks 14–18: maintain six priority keys; begin smoother two-hand preparation where comfortable.
Weeks 19–22: selected scales hands together slowly if relaxed.
Weeks 23–26: all 12 keys surveyed hands separately; priority keys most fluent.

### Chord changes
Month 1: C/F/G/Am whole-note changes without stopping.
Month 2: 1–4–5 and 1–5–6–4 in C/G/F/D at 60 bpm.
Month 3: common progressions in six priority keys with close inversions.
Month 4: seventh chords and 2–5–1 in C/F/G.
Month 5: passing movement without disturbing destination chords.
Month 6: progression circuit in at least C/G/F/D/A/Bb.

### Coordination
- hold LH root while RH plays five-finger pattern
- LH root/fifth + RH chord
- inversion changes with minimal hand travel
- melody + selected inner chord tones
- intro → main texture → ending without stopping

### Pulse
Use a metronome only as a diagnostic and stabilizer, not as a badge.
Targets:
- Week 2: 60 bpm whole-note chord changes
- Week 8: 60 bpm rhythmic accompaniment
- Week 13: progression circuit without pauses
- Week 25: difficult transitions 10–20 bpm below target, then return to performance tempo

## Clean-repetition rule
A benchmark is passed only after 3 clean repetitions on separate attempts, not one lucky run.

## Tension stop rule
Stop and reset if any of these appear:
- wrist/forearm tightness
- raised shoulders
- painful thumb crossing
- repeated finger collapse
- tempo increases but sound/control worsens

## Final practical target
By Week 26 the learner should have a relaxed practical base: common scales, major/minor triads, inversions, selected sevenths, several progressions, stable accompaniment and enough coordination to support simple choruses.
