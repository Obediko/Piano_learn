# Repertoire Execution Guide

Use this alongside `REPERTOIRE.md`. It defines how every assigned chorus or song should be learned without turning the course into tutorial copying.

## Five levels of mastery

- **Level 1:** melody or bass roots only.
- **Level 2:** left-hand roots + right-hand simple triads.
- **Level 3:** inversions + steady accompaniment.
- **Level 4:** melody plus chords or rhythmic accompaniment.
- **Level 5:** selective sevenths, sus/add9, passing movement and gospel colour.

Always keep the simpler version playable even after learning a richer version.

## Learning protocol for every piece

1. Identify the home key.
2. Play or sing the melody without chords.
3. Write the main chords as scale degrees/numbers.
4. Play left-hand roots only.
5. Add right-hand root-position triads.
6. Replace large jumps with inversions.
7. Add the weekly technique or rhythm skill.
8. Close the tutorial and play from memory.
9. Record one complete take.
10. Score it using `ASSESSMENTS.md`.

## Month 1

Use the four early choruses already listed in `REPERTOIRE.md`.

Technical objectives:
- Week 1: C/F/G keyboard geography and simple I-IV-V harmony.
- Week 2: steady beat and left-root/right-triad coordination.
- Week 3: melody confidence and hand independence.
- Week 4: C-Am-F-G and major/minor awareness.

By the Month 1 test, at least two pieces should reach `Basic` or better.

## Month 2

Repertoire objectives:
- one piece in G to establish scale-degree thinking;
- one slow lyrical piece for pedal control;
- one piece for inversion practice;
- one piece in D for rhythmic accompaniment.

By the Month 2 test, at least four pieces should be playable from memory in simplified form.

## Month 3

Repertoire objectives:
- 1-5-6-4 and 6-4-1-5 application;
- number-system thinking rather than letter-name dependence;
- dynamic phrasing;
- fuller left-hand patterns;
- a four-song mini-set.

By Week 13, perform four pieces with no tutorial open.

## Month 4

Revisit familiar pieces to add:
- dominant 7, minor 7 and major 7 colours;
- one walk-up or walk-down;
- 2-5-1 cadential movement;
- sus/add9 colour;
- deliberate intros and endings.

Rule: decoration is allowed only after the plain version is reliable.

## Month 5

At least two repertoire projects must now be learned partly by ear.

For each by-ear project, before checking a tutorial:
- find tonic;
- find opening melody note;
- identify likely bass roots;
- guess the number progression;
- record what you guessed;
- then check and correct.

One previously learned piece should also be transposed into at least three keys.

## Month 6

Final projects:
- one piece transposed through C/F/G/D;
- one arranged piece with intro, two textures and ending;
- one melody-plus-chords piece;
- one piece learned substantially by ear;
- a five-piece performance set.

## Retention system

After introducing any new piece:
- **Week +1:** play it twice during the week.
- **Week +2:** play it once from memory.
- **Week +3:** play it once with one deliberate improvement.
- **Week +4:** perform it on Saturday without tutorial.

Then move it into the rotating repertoire pool.

## Repertoire status labels

- `Learning`: still dependent on tutorial/chord sheet.
- `Basic`: can finish with simple harmony.
- `Secure`: from memory, steady pulse, no major stops.
- `Performance-ready`: intentional beginning/ending, can recover after mistake, can start from more than one section.

## Saturday repertoire rule

Every Saturday:
1. current week's piece;
2. one piece from previous 2 weeks;
3. one older piece from the rotating pool;
4. one complete recording.

This prevents early pieces from disappearing as new material arrives.
