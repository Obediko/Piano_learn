# Piano Transfer Tests — Can You Make Music Without the Tutorial?

Every week must prove transfer beyond imitation.

## Core principle
Learn → drill → apply → close the tutorial → perform / transpose / find by ear.

## Weekly transfer categories
### Weeks 1–4 — independent recall
- find notes without labels
- play C/F/G/Am without diagrams
- perform at least one chorus from memory
- keep pulse after a mistake

### Weeks 5–8 — number-system transfer
- play 1–4–5 and 1–5–6–4 in more than one key
- identify scale degrees aloud
- move a simple progression C→G/F
- use pedal without relying on a demonstration video

### Weeks 9–13 — progression and key fluency
- play common progressions in C/G/F/D
- transpose one easy chorus
- choose a usable inversion rather than copying hand shape
- mini-set with no tutorial open

### Weeks 14–18 — harmonic function
- build seventh chords from structure
- hear and play 2–5–1 in C/F/G
- add one walk/passing move without losing form
- explain why the destination chord still matters

### Weeks 19–22 — ear and transposition
- melody plus chord support without covering the melody
- one chorus in three keys
- find tonic and first phrase partly by ear before checking a source
- maintain a plain version before adding gospel decoration

### Weeks 23–26 — arrangement and performance
- explain enriched chord structure
- create intro + two textures + ending
- five-song no-stop set
- start pieces from the middle
- final 10–15 minute one-take performance

## Score 0–5
5 = independent, musical, stable pulse
4 = independent with small hesitation
3 = completes after one restart/retry
2 = heavily dependent on notes/tutorial
1 = attempt does not hold together
0 = not attempted

## Final transfer standard
The learner can perform familiar material without a tutorial, transpose an easy chorus into multiple keys, identify likely tonic/bass movement by ear, and create a simple arrangement rather than only reproducing a teacher's exact version.
